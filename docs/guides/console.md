The web console is for looking around and the occasional admin action. For daily work, use [the CLI](cli.md). To set it up for your deployment, see [the console runbook](../runbooks/console.md).

# Using the console

Open `https://<your proxy>/console/` (locally `https://localhost:3443/console/`) and choose *Continue with single sign-on*. It uses the same identity provider as the CLI, and what you see depends on your roles. Browser sign-ins last 8 hours by default (never longer than the maximum certificate lifetime), or until you sign out or an admin locks you.

| Page | What you can do | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Your identity and roles, what you can reach (with a copy-paste `limen ssh` command), recent sessions, and **Continue where you left off**: the resources you used last, each with its command. **Needs you** lists what is waiting: access requests for you to decide (and yours that are still waiting), policy changes and access reviews with something for you, a console session about to end and, for admins, disabled accounts and resources without a connected worker. **Favourites** and **Saved views** (see below) sit under it. A favourite or recent resource is a card: its name and kind, a star to remove it, and one line to get in, the command with a copy button, or *Choose how to connect* when you may act as several logins, roles or users (that leads to the resource page, where each has its own command). Admins also get live counts: active sessions, users, locks, workers, policy version | everyone |
| **Resources** | Search and filter by kind and label (click a label chip; the URL is shareable, e.g. `?kind=postgres&label=env=staging`), and sort by name or **recently used** (admins also: busiest, or **needs attention** first). Every row you can open shows how to get in and when you last used it: a **Connect** column with the command and a button to copy it, or *Choose how to connect* (to the resource page's builder) when you may act as several logins, roles or users and a bare command would not run. Admins' table is already wide, so there the same is a copy button (or an arrow to the builder) beside the star, and only for resources their own roles open. Admins also see each resource's **errors in the last 24 hours** (refused attempts, statement errors, web 5xx, Kubernetes errors, with a tooltip saying which) and **status**: *reachable*, *offline* (workers are enrolled but none holds a tunnel) or *no worker* (none matches), and a **Needs attention** filter for those two, and for any resource with errors. The star on a row makes it a **favourite**; favourites are listed first, and **Favourites** narrows the list to just them. A resource page **leads with how to connect**: one command per login, role or user where the CLI takes one (`limen ssh deploy@host`, `limen db connect orders --db-role app`, `limen redis connect cache --user reader`), else one command. **Build your command** below it works for databases, MongoDB, Redis, search, RabbitMQ and Kafka: pick who to act as, a database or virtual host, and a local port, and the exact command updates (the choices are in the address, so it can be bookmarked or shared, and picking one updates the command where you are, without reloading or moving the page). It offers only what your own roles allow and shows what each identity may open; where policy allows any database, you type the name. Kubernetes and web apps show what your access covers. Below it, a strip shows its status (admins), when it was last used, and who is **on it now** with links to **Details** and, for SSH, **Watch**; links go to all its sessions and its audit trail. Admins can **End** a live session from the strip. The tabs are **Overview** (labels, and for admins the target, selector and workers), **Access** (admins and auditors: who can reach it; admins also get a box to check why a given person, bot or group can't, in the simulator) and **Activity** (its sessions). **Request access** appears when you can ask | everyone sees their own; admins see all |
| **Sessions** | Live and past sessions, filterable by user, resource, kind, people or bots, how they ended and a UTC time range. Open one to **replay the recording** (SSH, after the control plane verifies its hash), read **its statements and commands** (SQL, Redis, MongoDB, search, RabbitMQ, Kafka; no recording) or **its Kubernetes API requests** (one kubectl session per login and cluster). Live SSH sessions have **Watch it** ([watching](watching-sessions.md)). Admins can **end** a session with a reason **Related links** on a session (the resource, the person, similar sessions, the audit trail of that session), **Copy link** and, on a recording, **Copy link to this moment**; the timeline of a recording is **marked where the output paused for 30 seconds or more**. A session still open refreshes itself. Admins can **End several sessions** at once: each is ended, and audited, on its own with the one reason you give. | everyone sees their own; admins and auditors see all |
| **Access** | **Ask for access** that expires, see your requests, and (approvers) the queue **waiting for you** with the reason and what the requester did on that resource in the past 30 days. Admins and auditors also see **active grants** (admins can revoke) and everyone's requests. See [access requests](access-requests.md) | everyone |
| **Reviews** | Items assigned to you to keep or remove. Admins start a review, give back a withheld role and download a **signed evidence pack**. See [access reviews](access-reviews.md) | everyone sees their own; admins and auditors see all |
| **Controls** | The latest [self-test of Limen's own controls](controls-report.md): each check with its result and what it found (a skipped check says so and is not a pass), the run history, and a download of the **signed controls report**. Admins can run a self-test now | admins and auditors; only admins start a run |
| **Users** | Who has signed in, where they came from, their IdP groups and roles, and whether the IdP still vouches for them ([offboarding](offboarding.md)); filter by state (**enabled** or **disabled**), group and role; applied filters show as chips under the bar, each removable, with **Clear all**. **Disable** a user (signed out, can't log in, open sessions end within seconds) or **enable** them again, always with an audited reason. A disabled user can also be **deleted** (record only; sessions and audit history stay), and the IdP check does it by itself after 90 days (`--idp-purge-after`). A person is disabled by an admin, or by the IdP check when the IdP no longer vouches for them | admins |
| **Bots** | CI jobs that can log in (from `bot` documents): what each matches, last login and **CI run** (linked), and sessions. Open a bot's page (it exists before the bot's first login) to disable it. See [bots](bots.md) | admins |
| **Workers** | Enrolled proxies and workers and their tunnels; outstanding **join tokens** (who made them, when they expire; never their values); **create single-use join tokens** (shown once) and **revoke** a component. **Resources → Discovered** holds what workers found in the cloud until you accept it ([discovery](discovery.md)) Each worker lists the **resources it can serve**, a *quiet* flag when it was not seen for five minutes, and *cert expired* when its certificate ran out without renewal. **Revoke several** works like ending several sessions: one audit event each. | admins |
| **Integrations** | What this control plane replica is configured to talk to, read only: the identity provider, the CA key backend (KMS key IDs), each **audit sink** with its backlog (*Waiting*), when it last accepted a batch and its failed batches since the process started, trace export, and the access request webhook and Slack hosts. A URL is reduced to where it points and no secret is shown. Integrations are set when Limen is deployed, not here. Trace delivery isn't reported (the exporter is best effort). Per replica: the counts reset when it restarts | admins |
| **Audit trail** | The last 30 days of events (sign-ins, denials, locks, kills, revokes, token creation, policy changes), filterable by event (or prefix such as `auth.*`), user, people or bots, text in the details, session and UTC time range. Applied filters are removable chips, and a row's **trail** link lists its session's events. A **Kubernetes** tab lists every kubectl API request, filterable by cluster, verb, namespace, user and "refused or failed". The **search box** finds a command or statement and opens the recording at that moment ([search](audit-search.md)). It's a summary: the complete signed events live in your audit sinks | admins; auditors read-only |
| **Dashboards** | Built-in charts for 24 hours, 7 or 30 days: sessions over time (people and bots apart), sign-ins and denials, bot sessions by repository, busiest resources and people, session length, denial reasons, admin actions, plus security panels (most refused people, out-of-hours access, longest sessions, sessions without a recording) and reliability panels (quiet components, tunnels per worker, database, Kubernetes and web-app errors). Pick a view (Overview, Security, Reliability, Bots, Usage, Everything) or **Choose panels**. Every number links to the matching audit or sessions list with filters set. Drawn by the console itself | admins |
| **Policy** | Every document as its YAML, with **its owner** (files from `limen admin apply`, the console, or a pipeline through the API). **Edit, create and delete the documents the console owns**; the rest are read-only. **Preview impact** on the edit and New document forms shows who would gain or lose access, the policy test results and whether the write would wait for approval, without saving | admins |
| **Access simulator** | One search box that suggests people, bots and IdP groups as you type (several groups separated by commas), and groups, bots and recent people as chips to click. The choices stay on the left and the result appears beside them, so nothing scrolls away. It shows what they can reach and could ask for, with the same decision code as enforcement. A person is looked up with the groups the IdP puts them in now. The address is shareable (`?person=`, `?bot=`, `?group=`). Changes nothing | admins |
| **Policy changes** | Writes held because they widen standing access: a pipeline's roles and mappings, or another admin's change under `--widening-approval=all`. Each shows the author, the CI run and who gains access. **Approve** applies it to the policy as it is now, or supersedes it if that would give access the page didn't show; **reject** needs a note. Never your own change, nor one from a CI run you started Each change shows **what it changes, document by document, against the active policy** (as the JSON the control plane holds). | admins, and approver groups of writer grants |

## Good to know

- **One owner per document.** What files define stays in Git: the console shows it read-only and `limen admin apply` changes it. What the console creates (source `console`, namespace `default`) it owns, and a file apply leaves it alone. Console writes go through the same checks as the API: owners, etags (a stale page is refused), policy tests with `--policy-tests=required`, and a second admin with `--widening-approval=all`.
- **Everything you change is audited** as you, marked as from the console.
- **Jump anywhere with `Ctrl+K` (`⌘K` on a Mac)**, or the *Jump to* button in the sidebar: type a page or a resource name (or a label), use the arrow keys and Enter. It lists only pages your menu already shows and resources the Resources page would show you. `g` then a letter goes to a page: `h` Overview, `r` Resources, `s` Sessions, `a` Audit trail, `u` Users, `w` Workers, `p` Policy (only the ones in your menu). `/` still focuses the search box. None of these work while you are typing in a box.
- **Favourites and saved views** are yours alone and follow you between browsers. Mark a resource as a favourite with its star (list or page); favourites are listed first on the Resources page; under any list page (**Resources, Sessions, Audit trail, Dashboards**) *Save this view as* keeps that page with its filters, period and chosen panels, and it appears on your Overview. You can keep 50. A saved view only remembers an address: the page it opens still checks what you may see, and a member can't save a page they can't open. Nothing here is audited, since it grants nothing.
- **Keyboard:** `j` and `k` mark the next and previous row of the table, `Enter` or `o` opens it.
- **The refresh line** under a refreshing page says when it last changed and lets you **Pause** or **Refresh now**.
- **Errors say what to do next** (go back, try again, ask for access).
- **Times read as "5 minutes ago"** for the last two weeks; hover for the exact UTC time. Older times, and everything without script, show the exact time.
- **List pages refresh every 10 seconds**, pausing while the tab is hidden, text is selected, anything has focus, or a form has unsaved input, so it never eats what you're typing.
- **The sidebar.** Pages are grouped: your everyday pages at the top (Overview, Resources, Sessions, Access), then **Approvals** (Policy changes and Reviews; shown to anyone who can open one of them, so a developer who only requests access never sees it), **Record** (Audit trail, Controls, and Dashboards for admins; admins and auditors) and **Administration** (Users, Bots, Workers, Policy, Access simulator, Integrations; admins). Click a group's name to fold it; the group holding the page you are on stays open. The button at the top of the sidebar shrinks it to icons (hover or focus an icon for its name) with a short animation, and the page takes the full width. When the menu is taller than the window, a soft shadow at its top and bottom edge shows there is more in that direction and disappears when you reach the end. Moving between pages fades the new content in with a small rise while the sidebar stays still, and cards and the first rows of a table rise into place one after another (browsers that support view transitions; others change page as before), and none of it animates if your system asks for reduced motion. The menu keeps its scroll position from page to page, and the theme and sign-out controls stay in view when it is longer than the window. The folds and the collapse are remembered in your browser; on a phone the menu is a single strip and neither applies.
- **Theme.** The sun/moon buttons at the bottom of the sidebar switch it; the default follows your system.
- **Mobile.** The menu becomes a strip that scrolls sideways, wide tables scroll inside their own box, and controls are thumb-sized.

**Browsers.** The current and previous major release of Chrome, Edge, Safari (macOS, iOS, iPadOS), Firefox and Firefox ESR, and Chrome on Android. The styling needs `color-mix()` and `:has()`: Chrome and Edge 111, Safari 16.2, Firefox 121. Older browsers drop what they don't understand, so some colour is lost (not tried). On 2026-11-03 the 18 pages a dev stack with data can show were run at widths from 360px to 1440px (nine engine and size profiles), in light and dark, in Chromium and WebKit (Playwright) and Firefox 159 on macOS (Puppeteer): no sideways scrolling, JavaScript errors or axe-core (WCAG 2.1 A and AA) violations, after one fix (the dashboards panel chooser ran 3px past the screen at 360px). On touch screens every control is at least 44px, measured in the Chromium phone and tablet profiles and the WebKit iPhone and iPad profiles on 2026-11-04 and checked in CI from then on; a mouse at a narrow width keeps the denser sizes. On 2026-11-10 the check also ran at window widths of 320, 480, 600, 768, 899, 901, 1280 and 1920px and at 1280x600 (Chromium, and WebKit at 320, 768 and 1024px), and over 23 pages including a finished session with its recording player, the Integrations page and the access simulator; that found and fixed a sideways scroll on Integrations at 320px, link text told apart only by colour, and touch targets under 44px in the simulator and the recording player. The recording in that check is a short invented one, and the session was a real SSH session on a dev stack built from this branch (templates unchanged). Not tried: Firefox ESR, Firefox below 159, Firefox with touch emulation (no engine here offers it), older engines, real devices, screen readers, and the pages that need data this stack did not have (access request and review detail, bot runs, Kubernetes requests, discovered resources, live watch). The same check runs in CI on saved pages ([runbook](../runbooks/console.md#checking-phones-and-other-browsers)).

**Command lists.** SQL, Redis, MongoDB and search sessions have no terminal recording. The session page lists what ran (up to 500 entries, including ones policy refused). The control plane keeps that text for 30 days (table `db_statements`), visible only to admins and the session's own user. The complete trail stays in S3 and Datadog.
