The web console is for looking around and the occasional admin action. For daily work, use [the CLI](cli.md). To set it up for your deployment, see [the console runbook](../runbooks/console.md).

# Using the console

Open `https://<your proxy>/console/` (locally `https://localhost:3443/console/`) and choose *Continue with single sign-on*. It uses the same identity provider as the CLI, and what you see depends on your roles. Browser sign-ins last 8 hours by default (never longer than the maximum certificate lifetime), or until you sign out or an admin locks you.

| Page | What you can do | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Overview** | Your identity and roles, what you can reach (with a copy-paste `limen ssh` command), recent sessions. Admins also get live counts: active sessions, users, locks, workers, policy version | everyone |
| **Resources** | Search and filter by kind and label (click a label chip; the URL is shareable, e.g. `?kind=postgres&label=env=staging`). A resource page shows labels and, for admins, its target, **which roles grant access**, **which live workers can serve it** (a red *none* means sessions will fail) and its sessions. **Request access** appears when you can ask; admins and auditors get **Who can reach it** | everyone sees their own; admins see all |
| **Sessions** | Live and past sessions, filterable by user, resource, kind, people or bots, how they ended and a UTC time range. Open one to **replay the recording** (SSH, after the control plane verifies its hash), read **its statements and commands** (SQL, Redis, MongoDB, search, RabbitMQ, Kafka; no recording) or **its Kubernetes API requests** (one kubectl session per login and cluster). Live SSH sessions have **Watch it** ([watching](watching-sessions.md)). Admins can **end** a session with a reason | everyone sees their own; admins and auditors see all |
| **Access** | **Ask for access** that expires, see your requests, and (approvers) the queue **waiting for you** with the reason and what the requester did on that resource in the past 30 days. Admins and auditors also see **active grants** (admins can revoke) and everyone's requests. See [access requests](access-requests.md) | everyone |
| **Reviews** | Items assigned to you to keep or remove. Admins start a review, give back a withheld role and download a **signed evidence pack**. See [access reviews](access-reviews.md) | everyone sees their own; admins and auditors see all |
| **Users** | Who has signed in, and their roles. **Lock** a user (signed out, can't log in, open sessions end within seconds) or **unlock** them, always with an audited reason | admins |
| **Bots** | CI jobs that can log in (from `bot` documents): what each matches, last login and **CI run** (linked), and sessions. Lock one from its page. See [bots](bots.md) | admins |
| **Workers** | Enrolled proxies and workers and their tunnels; outstanding **join tokens** (who made them, when they expire; never their values); **create single-use join tokens** (shown once) and **revoke** a component. **Resources → Discovered** holds what workers found in the cloud until you accept it ([discovery](discovery.md)) | admins |
| **Audit trail** | The last 30 days of events (sign-ins, denials, locks, kills, revokes, token creation, policy changes), filterable by event (or prefix such as `auth.*`), user, people or bots, text in the details, session and UTC time range. Applied filters are removable chips, and a row's **trail** link lists its session's events. A **Kubernetes** tab lists every kubectl API request, filterable by cluster, verb, namespace, user and "refused or failed". The **search box** finds a command or statement and opens the recording at that moment ([search](audit-search.md)). It's a summary: the complete signed events live in your audit sinks | admins; auditors read-only |
| **Dashboards** | Built-in charts for 24 hours, 7 or 30 days: sessions over time (people and bots apart), sign-ins and denials, bot sessions by repository, busiest resources and people, session length, denial reasons, admin actions, plus security panels (most refused people, out-of-hours access, longest sessions, sessions without a recording) and reliability panels (quiet components, tunnels per worker, database, Kubernetes and web-app errors). Pick a view (Overview, Security, Reliability, Bots, Usage, Everything) or **Choose panels**. Every number links to the matching audit or sessions list with filters set. Drawn by the console itself | admins |
| **Policy** | Every document as its YAML, with **its owner** (files from `limen admin apply`, the console, or a pipeline through the API). **Edit, create and delete the documents the console owns**; the rest are read-only. **Check a change**: paste YAML, see the diff, who gains or loses access (the **access simulator**) and policy test results; nothing is applied | admins |
| **Policy changes** | Writes held because they widen standing access: a pipeline's roles and mappings, or another admin's change under `--widening-approval=all`. Each shows the author, the CI run and who gains access. **Approve** applies it to the policy as it is now, or supersedes it if that would give access the page didn't show; **reject** needs a note. Never your own change, nor one from a CI run you started | admins, and approver groups of writer grants |

## Good to know

- **One owner per document.** What files define stays in Git: the console shows it read-only and `limen admin apply` changes it. What the console creates (source `console`, namespace `default`) it owns, and a file apply leaves it alone. Console writes go through the same checks as the API: owners, etags (a stale page is refused), policy tests with `--policy-tests=required`, and a second admin with `--widening-approval=all`.
- **Everything you change is audited** as you, marked as from the console.
- **List pages refresh every 10 seconds**, pausing while the tab is hidden, text is selected, anything has focus, or a form has unsaved input, so it never eats what you're typing.
- **Theme.** The sun/moon buttons at the bottom of the sidebar switch it; the default follows your system.
- **Mobile.** The menu becomes a strip that scrolls sideways, wide tables scroll inside their own box, and controls are thumb-sized.

**Browsers.** The current and previous major release of Chrome, Edge, Safari (macOS, iOS, iPadOS), Firefox and Firefox ESR, and Chrome on Android. The styling needs `color-mix()` and `:has()`: Chrome and Edge 111, Safari 16.2, Firefox 121. Older browsers drop what they don't understand, so some colour is lost (not tried). On 2026-11-03 the 18 pages a dev stack with data can show were run at widths from 360px to 1440px (nine engine and size profiles), in light and dark, in Chromium and WebKit (Playwright) and Firefox 159 on macOS (Puppeteer): no sideways scrolling, JavaScript errors or axe-core (WCAG 2.1 A and AA) violations, after one fix (the dashboards panel chooser ran 3px past the screen at 360px). On touch screens every control is at least 44px, measured in the Chromium phone and tablet profiles and the WebKit iPhone and iPad profiles on 2026-11-04 and checked in CI from then on; a mouse at a narrow width keeps the denser sizes. Not tried: Firefox ESR, Firefox below 159, Firefox with touch emulation, older engines, real devices, screen readers, and the pages that need a live session (session detail, recording, watch). The same check runs in CI on saved pages ([runbook](../runbooks/console.md#checking-phones-and-other-browsers)).

**Command lists.** SQL, Redis, MongoDB and search sessions have no terminal recording. The session page lists what ran (up to 500 entries, including ones policy refused). The control plane keeps that text for 30 days (table `db_statements`), visible only to admins and the session's own user. The complete trail stays in S3 and Datadog.
