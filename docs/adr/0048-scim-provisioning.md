# ADR-0048: SCIM 2.0 provisioning from the IdP

- Status: Accepted (the owner asked for pre-provisioning from Okta on 2026-11-10, reversing the SCIM non-goal of ADR-0047 for this step). Built against Okta's published SCIM 2.0 requests; **unverified against a real Okta (#64)**.
- Date: 2026-11-10

## Context
ADR-0047 made the IdP the source of truth for who may use Limen, but a person only exists in Limen after their first sign-in, and an IdP group change that the ID token does not carry (an IdP that leaves out a `groups` claim for people in none of its filtered groups) is not seen. A large organisation wants the opposite order: assign someone to the Limen app in Okta, and they show up in Limen, with the access their groups give, before they ever sign in; remove them and Limen knows within seconds.

## Decision
- **Limen serves a SCIM 2.0 endpoint** for Okta's provisioning agent: `/scim/v2/Users` and `/scim/v2/Groups` (Okta's "Push Groups"), plus `/ServiceProviderConfig`. It is **off unless `--scim-token` (`LIMEN_SCIM_TOKEN`, at least 32 characters) is set**. Only the requests Okta sends are built: user list-with-filter, create, get, replace, patch (activate or deactivate), and delete; group list-with-filter, create, get, replace, patch (rename, add, remove or replace members) and delete. No bulk, no sorting, no `/Schemas` or `/ResourceTypes`.
- **Authentication is a bearer token** (`Authorization: Bearer ...`), compared in constant time as SHA-256 digests, never logged or audited, wrapped in `Secret`. This is the one place Limen accepts a credential that is not an mTLS client certificate or an IdP-signed token; the mTLS invariant holds on the control plane's own listener because the endpoint is reached only through a proxy, which relays `/scim/` as it relays `/console/` (TLS terminates at the proxy; the relay strips `x-limen-*` headers). A wrong or missing token gets `401`, counted in `limen_scim_requests_total{outcome="unauthorized"}` and logged without the token.
- **A SCIM record grants nothing by itself.** Roles still come from the ID token at sign-in (ADR-0006). A provisioned person is a row in `users` with `source = 'scim'`, `login_count = 0` (shown as "never signed in"), no subject until their first sign-in, and an epoch `last_login_at` so nothing that compares it to a time (access-deny lifting) reads provisioning as a sign-in. The first sign-in fills the row in, keeps `source = 'scim'`, and matches by username ignoring case.
- **Pushed groups are the IdP's report of group membership, kept apart from the token's.** Membership is stored (`scim_groups`, `scim_group_members`) and never copied into `users.idp_groups`, which the IdP check owns. The groups the console compares with a person's roles are computed: the groups their last token showed, minus every group SCIM pushes, plus the person's SCIM memberships. A group SCIM never pushes (an `Everyone` baseline, a bootstrap admin group) therefore still counts for them, and a group SCIM does push is decided by SCIM alone. The console shows "next sign-in: +role" before the first sign-in. Nothing is applied early (roles are fixed at sign-in); a group only counts if a `role_mapping` names it. Unlinking (deleting) a pushed group removes nobody from it in the IdP, so SCIM simply stops speaking for it and the token's groups are theirs again.
- **Deactivation is instant and uses the lock.** `active: false` locks the person as `system:scim` (audited `user.lock`, via `scim`) and ends their logins (`idp.offboard`); `active: true` lifts only a `system:scim` lock, never an admin's. Like the IdP check's lock, a SCIM lock also lifts when the person next signs in successfully, because that is the IdP vouching for them (a missed `active: true` push must not strand someone the IdP lets in); the instant refusal holds until then, and a deactivated person cannot obtain a token. An admin's lock never lifts that way. Removal from a pushed group (and a rename of one) ends the logins that relied on a role it gave, judged against the person's groups as above; being added to a group never ends anything. It is the backstop for IdPs whose refreshed tokens omit the claim, and needs the IdP check's stored logins (`--idp-token-key`).
- **Delete** (Okta does not send it for users) locks, then deletes the record through the same audited path as the console's Delete. Because Okta never deletes, a person SCIM deactivated is purged by the same 90-day rule as one the IdP check disabled (`--idp-purge-after`), which runs inside the IdP check job and so needs `--idp-token-key`; an admin's lock is still never purged.
- **Every write is audited** inside the transaction that makes it (`user.provision`, `user.groups_change`, `user.lock`, `user.unlock`, `user.delete`), so a change never stands without its event, and the audit fails closed.

## Consequences
- \+ Pre-provisioning, near-instant offboarding and group-change detection with any IdP that speaks SCIM, filtered by who is assigned to the app.
- \+ No new dependency; it is a small axum router.
- − **A new authenticated entry point on the public proxy**, with a long-lived bearer secret. Anyone holding the token can create, lock and unlock people (not grant roles, which only a `role_mapping` and a real sign-in can). Rotate it like a database password; it is outside the PostgreSQL and KMS blast radius only by being a separate secret.
- − Okta needs a publicly trusted certificate on the proxy; the development stack's internal CA will not do.
- − Two sources (token claims and SCIM) write `users.idp_groups`; the later one wins. Both are the IdP.
- − Unverified against Okta until #64. Entra and others differ in PATCH shapes (Entra sends `path` forms); the patch handler accepts both Okta's pathless form and `path`-keyed operations, but only Okta's are built against.

## Alternatives
- **Poll the Okta API** with an API token: Okta-specific, no push, no Dex or Entra equivalent.
- **Event hooks:** per-IdP, and unauthenticated by default.
- **Leave SCIM out:** what ADR-0047 did; pre-provisioning and instant group removal were then impossible.
